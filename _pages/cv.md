## MAE3240 Heat Transfer Portfolio
[Download my Homework PDF]({{ "/Homework_7.pdf" | relative_url }})

The most valuable homework I completed was homework 7, due to its relevancy to the work I do outside of the course. The assignment included a cylinder in crossflow with internal convection - this is conceptually similar to my project team design, a motor cooling sleeve. The motor cooling sleeve is a cylinder that encapsulates the motor, running water over it. It is also exposed to the air in crossflow, with a varying u infinity term as the vehicle changes speed. Additionally, it models the external fluid as the cold fluid and the internal fluid as the hot fluid, similarly mimicking the setup of the design on the car. Lastly, the question asks us to calculate the mean temperature of fluid A at the outlet Tm,o and to sketch the mean temperature
profile as a function of x coordinate. One of the most important design aspects for the cooling sleeve is that the fluid temperature in contact with the motor does not go above a certain limit, so knowing how to calculate the outlet temperature and the mean temperature profile is crucial to the design process.

The problem set’s relation to a highly applicable mechanical design made it particularly significant. While oftentimes, problem sets can feel separated from real world applications this course has repeatedly highlighted real use cases. This makes the problem sets feel particularly exciting and important. While this has been consistent throughout the course, having a question so closely related to my design was very motivating and exciting. Based on what we learned in class, I then went through and compared the calculation outputs against the physical outputs of my design on the car. While the heat transfer concepts are not 1 to 1, it was interesting to compare the real world with the concepts from class.
